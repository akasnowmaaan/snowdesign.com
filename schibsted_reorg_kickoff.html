Planning a workshop for a Schibsted developer team reorganization

Schibsted was reorganizing to place all the developers for the core product in one team. I created and facilitated the workshop to start the new team right.

Background

The Schibsted publishing developer product teams were previously siloed to focus on individual offerings, resulting in duplicated effort, technical debt, a fractured customer experience, and rock-bottom morale. Schibsted as a whole was restructuring around centralized platforms to address these problems, so the developers were moving to one 'Core' organization.

The directors of the new Core org worked with developers at all levels to create a set of 'team-facing working principles' to help the newly-combined team work as one. They just needed to start the new org by demonstrating to the dispirited group that the leadership had listened, learned, and changed. That's when I joined.

Kicking it off

Mark Pasternak and Anders Berg were planning a single-day offsite. I was asked to help, due to my experience as a trained workshop planner and facilitator.

The first half of the day was spoken for walking through the reorg, showing the new working principles, and holding a Q&A.

The second half of the day was my mine - a workshop to get the new teams aligned on a clear set of priorities, and fired up about starting work on them first thing Monday morning.

This was a challenging event. We've all been to an 'off-site' where everyone blows a day moving post-it notes around and brainstorming dozens of product ideas that never get mentioned again. Some may find those fun, but I know that's torture for developers, especially this crew. I worked with them long enough to know how many useless off-sites they suffered through.

They craved a practical use of their time. This event needed to be practical, effective, and above all, have tangible results.

Approach

My core objectives:

  • Drive the event using the new principles - to drive home that they matter
  • Jump-start open, honest, productive conversations among members of the new teams
  • Make sure everyone knew every moment spent will lead directly to an actionable outcome

The structure:

  • Present an activity and a model of what success looks to the entire group
  • Break out into groups of 5-6 people with each led by a facilitator to do the activity
  • Take turns talking to the the other groups to share work and compare notes

With any luck, I'd run an offsite workshop that even an engineer would love.

The Workshop

The first half of the workshop used the model of 'brainstorm broadly, collate/de-dupe, then sort into themes'. This led to everyone seeing all the issues, and coming to a shared understanding what areas required their effort, and which ones they felt empowered to say 'no' to.

The second half of the workshop took those themes and identified the interdependencies. That led to insights such as 'If we solve issue X, we actually eliminate issue Y and Z for free', so the final step of prioritization was made with contextual understanding.

To end the day, the groups came back together and took turns sharing their findings to the whole room. The ultimate deliverable was a list of known issues grouped by theme, with the top three overall priorities called out for immediate attention.

After the workshop, us facilitators and planners digitized all the priorities into the Kanban, so Monday morning all the participants would get to work on the agenda they created.

Results

It was a resounding success. I make it a point to pay attention to the general sentiment, and instead of hearing grumbling about how much time was wasted, or frustration at the prospect of a reorg, I heard people taking charge and getting excited about the plans they were making.

Most importantly, I didn't see a single developer sitting off to the side with a laptop open to their git repository. They all participated, and the feedback was united in saying the day was time well spent.
